The Rights Ledger

Every film on Jolly Good Show has a page here showing why we believe it is in the public domain in the United States, which print we used and where it came from, and what stays protected even though the film is free. We keep this ledger because “it’s on the internet” is not evidence, and because you deserve to know.

How we check

  • Published before 1931? Then its US copyright has run out, full stop. (Films published in 1931 join them on January 1, 2027.)
  • Published 1931 to 1963? Under the 1909 Copyright Act a film got 28 years, then had to be renewed or it expired. We search the U.S. Copyright Office’s own renewal records, the Catalog of Copyright Entries, for the years each renewal would have been filed, by title and by studio. A film enters this ledger only if no renewal turns up.
  • The print matters too. A modern restoration or a new music track can carry its own copyright, so we use older film and TV transfers with their original titles and soundtrack, and we say which copies we turned down.
  • Some things never come free with the film: studio trademarks, famous characters used as branding, and sometimes the songs. We note those on each page.

Last updated October 20, 2026. This is our research, not legal advice, and it covers the United States only; other countries have different rules. If you think we’ve got one wrong, tell us in the comments on the video and we’ll look again.
